## Study Notes

Standard Real Book changes, following Hancock's *Maiden Voyage* (1965). Lead sheets often write the sus chords as slash chords: `Am7/D` = `D7sus4`, `Cm7/F` = `F7sus4`, `Bbm7/Eb` = `Eb7sus4`. Hancock's bridge chord is often written `C#m11`.

The tune has **no ii–V–Is at all**: four chords, each held for four bars. The challenge is making long static harmony move.

### A sections

**Bars 1–4 (D7sus4):** **D Mixolydian** — or think **A Dorian** / **C major pentatonic from D** (the `Am7/D` spelling). Avoid the major 3rd (F#): it undoes the sus sound. Target the 4th (G), 9th (E) and b7 (C).

**Bars 5–8 (F7sus4):** Up a **minor third**. **F Mixolydian** / **C Dorian** / **Eb major pentatonic**. Common tone with D7sus4: C (b7 of D, 5 of F) — use it to connect the two areas.

### Bridge

**Bars 17–20 (Eb7sus4):** A half step above the A section's D7sus4. **Eb Mixolydian** / **Bb Dorian**.

**Bars 21–24 (C#m7):** The only minor chord, and the darkest color in the tune. **C# Dorian** (or C# Aeolian). It sits a half step below D, setting up the return to D7sus4 by half step.

### Practice strategy

1. **One pentatonic per chord:** a major pentatonic built on the b7 of each sus chord outlines the sound perfectly — **C** over D7sus4, **Eb** over F7sus4, **Db** over Eb7sus4. Over C#m7 use **C# minor pentatonic** (= E major pentatonic).
2. **Quartal voicings:** stack 4ths from the 5th of each sus chord (on D7sus4: `A D G C`). Move the same shape between chords.
3. **Motivic playing:** with four bars per chord, develop one idea and transpose it to each new chord instead of running scales.
4. **Straight eighths:** don't swing it. Lock in with the recording's ostinato rhythm.
