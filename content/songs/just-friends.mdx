## Study Notes

Standard Real Book changes; the reference is Charlie Parker's *Charlie Parker with Strings* (1950).

The tune **starts on IV, not I**, and gets home through the **backdoor**: `CMaj7 → Cm7 → F7 → GMaj7` is IV → iv → bVII7 → I. The tonic doesn't arrive until bar 5, and when the tune seems about to settle, a chromatic ii–V (`Bbm7 Eb7`) slides it into Am7.

### A — the backdoor home

**Bars 1–2 (CMaj7):** IV of G. **C Lydian** (F#, the leading tone of G) makes it clear this is not the tonic.

**Bars 3–4 (Cm7 / F7):** **Backdoor ii–V**: iv–bVII7 → I. Both chords share **F Mixolydian** (= C Dorian). Guide tones: `E → Eb → Eb → D` — the 3rd of CMaj7 falls to the 3rd of Cm7, stays as the 7th of F7, then lands on D (5 of G).

**Bars 5–6 (GMaj7):** Tonic. **G major**.

**Bars 7–8 (Bbm7 / Eb7):** ii–V a half step above Am7 — really a **tritone substitute** for the `Em7 A7` that would lead to Am7 (Eb7 is the tritone sub of A7). **Bb Dorian** / **Eb Mixolydian** or **Eb Lydian dominant** (A natural).

### B — cycle of dominants

**Bars 9–10 (Am7 / D7):** ii–V in G, but it doesn't resolve.

**Bars 11–12 (Bm7 / E7):** iii then V/ii. **B Phrygian** (from G) → **E Mixolydian b9 b13** (the G natural and C natural keep it close to G).

**Bars 13–14 (A7):** V/V for two bars. **A Mixolydian**; C# is the new note. Try **A Lydian dominant** (D#) for bar 14.

**Bars 15–16 (Am7 / D7):** ii–V back to CMaj7 at the top of A — D7 resolves *deceptively* to IV instead of I.

### C — the real resolution

**Bars 25–28:** Same as the B section start.

**Bars 29–31 (Am7 / D7 / GMaj7):** The only full ii–V–I in G in the tune — resolution to the tonic.

**Bar 32 (Am7 D7):** Turnaround — resolves back to CMaj7 (deceptive again) for the next chorus.

### Practice strategy

1. **Backdoor first:** loop bars 1–5 until `Cm7 F7 → G` sounds like a cadence, not a surprise.
2. **One scale for bars 3–4:** F Mixolydian covers both chords — practice lines that land on D or B in bar 5.
3. **Tritone sub drill:** play bars 7–9 as written, then as `Em7 A7 | Am7`, and compare.
4. **Bird's approach:** this is a bebop staple. Parker's lines use chromatic approach notes into chord tones on the downbeats.
