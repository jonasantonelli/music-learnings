## Study Notes

Chord changes follow Coltrane's recording on *Giant Steps* (1960). Some charts end bar 16 on `Eb7(#11)` instead of `A7`.

### Tune Up underneath

Countdown is a contrafact of Miles Davis's **Tune Up**, which is three ii–V–Is descending by whole step: `D major → C major → Bb major`. Coltrane keeps each **ii** and each **I**, and fills the space between them with the Giant Steps cycle (keys a major third apart).

### How each four-bar phrase works

Take bars 1–4 (target **DMaj7**):

- **Em7** — the original ii of D.
- **F7 → BbMaj7** — a V–I a half step above the ii.
- **Db7 → GbMaj7** — next key, a major third down.
- **A7 → DMaj7** — lands on the original tonic, another major third down.

So each phrase visits `(ii) → Bb → Gb → D`, all a major third apart. The same shape repeats:

**Bars 5–8 (target CMaj7):** `Dm7 → Eb7 AbMaj7 → B7 EMaj7 → G7 CMaj7`. Keys: `Ab → E → C`.

**Bars 9–12 (target BbMaj7):** `Cm7 → Db7 GbMaj7 → A7 DMaj7 → F7 BbMaj7`. Keys: `Gb → D → Bb`.

**Bars 13–16:** The cycle stops. `Em7 | F7 | BbMaj7 | A7` gives each chord a full bar. Treat F7 → BbMaj7 as a plain V–I, and A7 as the turnaround back to Em7 (the V of the ii).

### Scale choices

Each dominant–major pair is one key: on `F7 BbMaj7`, play **Bb major** throughout. On the ii chords, play **Dorian** (E Dorian, D Dorian, C Dorian). At tempo, the **1-2-3-5 pattern** on each chord (as on Giant Steps) is the classic way through.

### Practice strategy

1. **Play Tune Up first:** `Em7 A7 | DMaj7 | Dm7 G7 | CMaj7 | Cm7 F7 | BbMaj7` — hear the targets.
2. **Add the cycle:** keep landing on the same tonics while passing through the two new keys.
3. **Target the 3rd of each Maj7:** `F# → E → D` (3rds of D, C, Bb) — the skeleton of each phrase.
4. **Compare with Giant Steps:** same keys, different starting points.
