## Study Notes

Changes follow the *Kind of Blue* (1959) recording, the standard lead-sheet version. The form is a single **10-bar** chorus that cycles continuously — no bridge, no turnaround back to a clear tonic.

**Bar 1 (BbMaj7):** Opens on bVI of D minor. **Bb Lydian** (the E natural is the #11 and the 9th of Dm). Evans voices this as `BbMaj7(#11)`.

**Bar 2 (A7(#9)):** V of D minor. **A altered** or **A half-whole diminished** for the #9. Guide tones: `A → G` (7th of BbMaj7 to 7th of A7) and `D → C#` (3rd of BbMaj7 to 3rd of A7).

**Bar 3 (Dm7 Db7):** Arrival on the tonic, then **Db7** — a tritone substitute for G7, sliding chromatically into Cm7. **D Dorian**, then **Db Lydian dominant**.

**Bar 4 (Cm7 F7):** ii–V to Bb. **C Dorian / F Mixolydian** (one scale: Bb major).

**Bar 5 (BbMaj7):** Resolution, briefly heard as a new tonic. **Bb major** or **Bb Lydian**.

**Bar 6 (A7(b13)):** V of D minor again. **D harmonic minor from A** (A Phrygian dominant) — the b9 (Bb) and b13 (F) come straight from the previous bar.

**Bar 7 (Dm7):** Tonic. **D Dorian** or **D Aeolian**.

**Bar 8 (E7(#9)):** V of A minor. **E altered**. Pushes the harmony *away* from D.

**Bars 9–10 (Am7 / Dm7):** **A Aeolian** then **D Dorian**. Dm7 goes straight back to BbMaj7 at the top, so the tune never feels finished — that's the point.

### Practice strategy

1. **Learn the root line by ear:** `Bb A D-Db C-F Bb A D E A D`. Sing it with the recording until the 10-bar cycle feels natural.
2. **Guide tones:** write out the 3rd and 7th of every chord — they mostly move by half step, which is what makes the unusual root motion sound smooth.
3. **Voicings over scales:** at ballad tempo, focus on rootless voicings and melody. Evans's left-hand voicings on the record are the best study material.
4. **Double time:** once the form is internalized, run the changes at twice the speed. With a new chord every bar (or half bar), 10 bars go by fast.
