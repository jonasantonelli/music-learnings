## Study Notes

Naima is a ballad from Coltrane's *Giant Steps* (1959), dedicated to his first wife. The form is short (20 bars AABA, with A only 4 bars), but the harmony is unlike anything else in the standard repertoire — almost every chord sits over a **pedal bass** (Bb on the A sections, Eb on the bridge). The piece predates the major-thirds cycle of [[have-you-met-miss-jones]] by months but already shows Coltrane thinking in non-functional, symmetrical key relationships.

### A section — the Bb pedal

The bass stays on Bb for all four bars while quartal/sus-flavored chords float above it. Don't read these slash chords as functional triads — read them as **colors over Bb**.

**Bar 1 (AbMaj7/Bb):** Ab major triad (Ab–C–Eb–G) over Bb bass. The Bb is the 9 of Ab, so this voicing is effectively `Bb13sus(no3)` — a suspended dominant that never resolves. Improvise with **Ab major** (= Bb Dorian = Bb Mixolydian sus). All three names describe the same notes; pick whichever framing helps you hear the bar.

**Bar 2 (BMaj7/Bb):** B major (B–D#–F#–A#) over Bb. The half-step rub between B and Bb is the whole point — this is the most dissonant bar in the song. Think **B Lydian** (F# is the 5 of B), or hear it as a chromatic neighbor to bar 1. The melody note here is a long Eb, which sits as the #11/b5 of B — Coltrane is using **B Lydian** explicitly.

**Bar 3 (BbMaj7):** The pedal finally aligns with its own tonic chord — Bb is now the root, not a foreign bass. Brief moment of stability. **Bb major** (or Bb Lydian for the Eb→E color carrying over from bar 2).

**Bar 4 (AbMaj7/Bb):** Back to the bar-1 voicing. The A section never "resolves" — it just breathes in and out around the Bb drone.

**Practice idea:** play just the bass (Bb whole notes for 4 bars) and sing each upper structure. You'll hear that A is a single sustained mode colored by three different upper triads.

### Bridge — Eb pedal, double-time harmonic rhythm

The bass shifts to Eb and the chords come twice as fast (one per bar, eight bars total). This is Coltrane's signature move: change the **harmonic rhythm**, not just the chords, to mark sections.

**Bars 9–12 (BMaj7/Eb · AbMaj7/Eb · BMaj7/Eb · AbMaj7/Eb):** Two upper triads alternating over Eb. Same trick as the A section — Eb is the 11 of B and the 5 of Ab, so neither chord "owns" the bass. The motion B↔Ab is a **minor third**, planting the seed of the augmented/major-third cycles Coltrane would develop in *Giant Steps*. Improvise by tracking the upper triad: **B Lydian** in bars 9 and 11, **Ab major** in bars 10 and 12.

**Bar 13 (GbMaj7/Eb):** Third upper triad enters — Gb–Bb–Db–F over Eb. Now you have a three-key cycle on the bridge: B → Ab → Gb (down minor thirds), still over the same Eb pedal. **Gb Lydian** or **Eb Dorian** (same notes).

**Bar 14 (BMaj7/Eb):** Back to the opening bridge color.

**Bar 15 (EMaj7):** **The pedal breaks.** For one bar the bass leaves Eb and the chord stands alone — EMaj7, a tritone away from Bb and a half-step above Eb. This is the most striking moment in the song: after seven bars of suspended pedal-point, a fully resolved major-7 chord appears in an unrelated key. **E major** or **E Lydian** — and lean into the surprise. Don't rush past it.

**Bar 16 (Fm7 · Bb7):** ii–V that sets up the return. On paper this is a ii–V of Eb, but the resolution lands on `AbMaj7/Bb` — which contains a Bb bass and Ab major notes (= the upper voicing of `Bb13sus`). So Bb7 doesn't resolve to Eb; it resolves to its own **sus form** at the top of the next A. **F Dorian** on Fm7, **Bb altered** or **Bb Mixolydian** on Bb7 — the alterations smear into the next bar's Bb pedal nicely.

### Improvisation approach

- **Think modal, not functional.** Naima is not a chord-by-chord blowing tune. Outline upper triads over a stable bass; let the pedal do the work of grounding the harmony.
- **Three triads on A, four on B.** That's it. A = Ab / B / Bb / Ab over Bb. B = B / Ab / Gb / E with the Eb pedal until the EMaj7 bar.
- **Voice-lead by half-step.** The melodic interest comes from the chromatic rub between upper triad and pedal (Ab→A, B→Bb, Gb→G, etc.). Target the half-step movement, not the chord tones.
- **Leave space.** It's a ballad. Coltrane plays sparsely on the head — the silences are part of the composition.
