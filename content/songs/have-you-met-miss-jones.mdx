## Study Notes

Chord changes follow Joe Pass's *Virtuoso* (1973) solo, with bar 2's `F#dim7` retained from the standard chart and an `Eb7` added to bar 17 of the bridge.

**Bar 1 (FMaj7):** Home base — **F major scale**. Target chord tones (F A C E); avoid Bb (the 4) on strong beats.

**Bar 2 (F#dim7):** Chromatic passing diminished — functions as a rootless `D7(b9)` setting up Gm7. Use **F# whole-half diminished** (same notes as the F#dim7 chord with passing tones), or simply think `D7(b9)` and play **D Mixolydian b9 b13** / **half-whole D dim**. The line should voice-lead `F# → G` into the next bar.

**Bar 3 (Gm7):** Diatonic ii — **G Dorian** (or F major scale from G). Watch the natural 6 (E) as a color tone, not a target.

**Bar 4 (C7):** Just the V — Pass replaces what's often written as a chromatic passing `G#dim7` (a rootless `E7(b9)` into Am7) with the dominant it implies. Use **C Mixolydian**, or **C altered** for tension before the long turnaround.

**Bars 5–8 (Am7 / Dm7 / Gm7 / C7):** A long **iii–vi–ii–V** — Pass's substitution for the typical pair of ii–Vs cycling back to F. The tonic doesn't return until bar 9, which builds forward motion. Everything is diatonic to F major, so you can improvise with **F major** across all four bars while targeting guide tones (`C → C → Bb → Bb`).

Per-bar: **Am7** = iii (F major from A). **Dm7** = vi (F major from D, the natural 6 of the parent key colors this bar). **Gm7** = ii (G Dorian). **C7** = V (C Mixolydian, or altered to push into the next chorus).

**Bars 13–16 (FMaj7 / Dm7 / Cm7 / F7):** Pass stretches the **pivot to the bridge across two bars** instead of cramming it into bar 16. Bars 13–14 coast on tonic (`FMaj7` then `Dm7` for color); bars 15–16 are the **ii–V into Bb** — `Cm7` (**C Dorian**) then `F7` (**F Mixolydian**, *not* F major — this is V/IV, not the tonic).

### Bridge — the famous major-thirds cycle

The bridge is a Rodgers innovation that pre-figures **Coltrane changes**: three key centers a major third apart (`Bb → Gb → D`), each set up by its own ii–V. Practice the bridge slowly until you can hear all three tonics as equally weighted.

**Bar 17 (BbMaj7 Eb7):** New tonic, then **Eb7** as V of Abm — pushes momentum into the minor ii–V instead of sitting on the tonic. Play **Bb major** (or Bb Lydian) on BbMaj7, then **Eb Mixolydian** (or Eb altered) on Eb7. Half-step voice leading `Eb → Db` and `G → Gb` sets up bar 18.

**Bar 18 (Abm7 Db7):** Minor ii–V to GbMaj7 — both chords share **Db Mixolydian** / **Ab Dorian** (same scale, Gb major one step ahead). Voice-lead chromatically: `Db → C` is a half-step landing on the 5 of Gb? No — `Db` is the 5 of Gb, so think `F → Gb` (the 7→root resolution from Db7).

**Bar 19 (GbMaj7):** New tonic, a **major third down** from BbMaj7. **Gb major scale** (or Gb Lydian).

**Bar 20 (Em7 A7):** ii–V to DMaj7. **A Mixolydian** / **E Dorian** — one scale across the bar.

**Bar 21 (DMaj7):** New tonic, a **major third down** from GbMaj7 (and a major third up from Bb — completing the augmented-triad cycle Bb–Gb–D). **D major scale**.

**Bar 22 (Abm7 Db7):** Back to the ii–V into GbMaj7. Same scale as bar 18.

**Bar 23 (GbMaj7):** Final bridge tonic.

**Bar 24 (Gm7 C7):** ii–V back home to F. The leap from GbMaj7 down to Gm7 is a half step — practice the smooth voice leading (`Bb → Bb`, `Db → C`, `Gb → G`, `F → F`).

### Practice strategy for the bridge

1. **Outline the three tonics:** play just `BbMaj7 — GbMaj7 — DMaj7` as whole notes, internalizing the major-third sound.
2. **Add the ii–Vs:** play each ii–V resolving to its target.
3. **Don't skip the Eb7:** its V → minor resolution into Abm7 is the engine of the bridge's first half — practice the half-step voice leading down.
4. **One scale per ii–V:** simplifies bridge improvisation — Db Mixolydian (bars 18, 22), A Mixolydian (bar 20), and C Mixolydian (bar 24).
5. **Guide-tone line:** target the 3rds and 7ths through the cycle — they move by half steps.

### A3 — final A with Pass's touches

**Bar 28 (C7 Bb7):** Pass tacks **Bb7** onto the end of the V — a bVII7 / blues color pulling chromatically toward `Am7` in bar 29.

**Bar 31 (FMaj7 Dm7):** I → vi instead of resting on the tonic — keeps motion into the final ii–V. (For a softer landing, substitute `F6` here — same trick as Days of Wine and Roses.)
