## Study Notes

Common lead-sheet changes in C, as played on *Getz/Gilberto* (1964). The second half of each 16-bar section varies between lead sheets; check against the recording.

The tune famously **doesn't start on the tonic**: C major is implied from bar 1 but only arrives in the final bars.

### A — the long way to C

**Bar 1 (D7/A):** **II7** (V/V) with the 5th in the bass. **D Lydian dominant** (G# as #11 matches Jobim's color). The A in the bass starts a descending chromatic line: `A → Ab → G`.

**Bar 2 (Abdim7):** Chromatic passing diminished between D7/A and Gm7 — its job is the bass step `Ab → G`. **Ab whole-half diminished**.

**Bars 3–4 (Gm7 C7):** ii–V to F. **G Dorian / C Mixolydian**.

**Bars 5–6 (FMaj7 → Fm6):** IV then **iv** (minor subdominant, borrowed from C minor). **F Lydian**, then **F melodic minor**. The `A → Ab` inner voice is the signature sound.

**Bars 7–8 (Em7 A7(b9)):** iii of C, then V/ii. **E Phrygian** (or E Dorian), then **A half-whole diminished**.

**Bars 9–12 (Dm7 G7 / Em7 A7):** ii–V in C, then the same iii–VI7 again — a delayed resolution.

**Bars 13–16 (D7 / Dm7 G7):** II7 again (**D Mixolydian**), turning minor (`F# → F`) into the ii–V that returns to the top.

### B — resolving at last

Bars 17–24 repeat the opening. **Bar 26 (Fm6)** brings back the minor subdominant before the final **iii–VI7–ii–V–I** (`Em7 A7(b9) Dm7 G7 C6`).

### Practice strategy

1. **Bass line:** play `A Ab G C F F E A` for the first 8 bars — the chromatic slide at the start and the `F → Fm` color are what make the tune.
2. **Guide tones in bars 5–6:** move only the 3rd `A → Ab` while the rest of the voicing stays still (`FMaj7` → `Fm6`: F A C E → F Ab C D).
3. **Resist resolving early:** don't play C major material until the final bars. Treat the A section as a tour of the subdominant and dominant areas.
