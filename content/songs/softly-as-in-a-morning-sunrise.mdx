## Study Notes

Chart follows common Real Book–style changes. Coltrane's *Live at the Village Vanguard* (1961) reading treats the A sections as an open C minor vamp — use it to hear how much space the harmony leaves. Check the bridge against the recording; lead sheets differ in bars 1–4 of the B.

### A sections — the C minor vamp

**Every odd bar (Cm7):** Tonic. **C Dorian** is the jazz default (A natural = bright minor); **C natural minor** is darker and closer to the melody.

**Every even bar (Dm7b5 G7(b9)):** Minor ii–V back to Cm7. **C harmonic minor** over the whole bar is the simplest choice; **G altered** on beats 3–4 adds more tension.

The A section is essentially one idea repeated four times — use it to build **motivic** solos: take a 2-bar phrase and develop it over the four repeats.

**A2, bar 8 (Gm7b5 C7(b9)):** The pivot. Cm7 turns into C7 — a V of Fm — to launch the bridge. **F harmonic minor** across the bar.

### B — bridge

**Bars 1–2 (Fm7 / Bb7):** ii–V to Eb. **Eb major** across both bars.

**Bar 3 (EbMaj7):** Relative major of C minor — the brightest moment in the tune.

**Bar 4 (AbMaj7):** IV of Eb (bVI of C minor) — **Ab Lydian** (D natural) links smoothly back to the C minor collection.

**Bars 5–7 (Dm7b5 / G7(b9) / Cm7):** Minor ii–V–i home, stretched to one chord per bar. Target B natural on G7(b9).

### Practice strategy

1. **Vamp the A section** with a backing loop and solo only with C Dorian → C harmonic minor, switching every bar.
2. **Hear the pivot:** play `Cm7 → C7(b9) → Fm7` slowly — the `Eb → E` rise is what opens the bridge.
3. **Compare with Autumn Leaves:** same minor ii–V–i vocabulary, but here it sits in one key for 8 bars instead of alternating with the relative major.
