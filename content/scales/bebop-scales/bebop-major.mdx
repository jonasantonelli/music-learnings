The **bebop major** scale is the major scale with a chromatic **♯5** added
between the 5th and the 6th. Like every bebop scale it has **eight** notes, so a
bar of straight eighth notes lines the chord tones up on the downbeats — but here
the target chord is a **major 6** (or maj7) rather than a dominant.

### Interval formula

| Degree | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | ♯5 | 6 | 7 |
|--------|---|---|---|---|---|----|---|---|
| Semitones from root | 0 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 7 | **8** | 9 | 11 |

The bold ♯5 is the passing tone. Everything else is the ordinary major scale.

### What it's played over

Use it over a **Imaj7** or **I6** chord — the tonic. Over `C6` or `Cmaj7`, play
**C bebop major**. The key chord tones it targets are those of the 6 chord:
**R, 3, 5, 6**.

### The rhythmic rule

Run it in eighth notes from a chord tone on beat 1 and the chord tones of the 6
chord (R, 3, 5, 6) land on the downbeats. Descending **C bebop major** over `C6`
from the root:

| Beat | 1 | & | 2 | & | 3 | & | 4 | & |
|------|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Note | C | B | A | G♯ | G | F | E | D |
| Role | **R** | 7 | **6** | *♯5* | **5** | 4 | **3** | 2 |

The chord tones **C, A, G, E** (R, 6, 5, 3) all fall on the beat; the chromatic
G♯ slots in on an upbeat to hold the alignment.

### Practice tips

- **Vamp on `C6` and descend from the root** in steady eighths until the chord
  tones-on-the-beat feeling is automatic.
- **Compare with bebop dominant** in the same key — the only difference is where
  the chromatic sits (♯5 here, ♮7 there) and which chord it targets.
- **Resolve a ii–V into it**: land on the tonic bar and continue the line with
  bebop major so the arrival chord tones stay on the beats.
- **Hear the ♯5 as a passing color, not a chord tone** — it should slide through,
  never be leaned on.
