The **bebop dorian** scale (also called the **bebop minor** scale) is Dorian with
a chromatic **natural 3rd** added between the ♭3 and the 4. It's the minor-chord
member of the bebop family, used over **ii-7** chords.

### Interval formula

| Degree | 1 | 2 | ♭3 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | ♭7 |
|--------|---|---|----|---|---|---|---|----|
| Semitones from root | 0 | 2 | 3 | **4** | 5 | 7 | 9 | 10 |

The bold ♮3 is the passing tone; the other seven notes are plain Dorian.

### The ii–V shortcut

Here's the most useful fact about this scale: **it contains exactly the same
eight notes as the bebop dominant a perfect 4th above its root.**

> **D bebop dorian** = D E F F♯ G A B C
> **G bebop dominant** = G A B C D E F F♯

Same collection, different starting note. Since `Dm7` is the **ii** of `G7`, this
means you can run **one eight-note scale across the whole `Dm7 – G7`** part of a
ii–V and the bebop chord-tone alignment carries through both bars. Many players
simply *think bebop dominant from the V* and let it cover the ii as well.

### What it's played over

Use it over a **ii-7** chord (or any Dorian-flavored minor 7th). Over `Dm7`, play
**D bebop dorian** — or equivalently think **G bebop dominant**.

### The rhythmic rule

Because it shares its notes with the related bebop dominant, the chord-tones-on-
the-downbeats property is inherited from that dominant. The cleanest way to feel
it is to run the line resolving toward the V: descending **G bebop dominant**
over `Dm7 – G7` lands the `G7` chord tones on the beats exactly as on the
[Bebop Dominant](/lessons/scales/bebop-scales/bebop-dominant) page, while the
`Dm7` bar is spelled from its own chord tones (D, F, A, C).

### Practice tips

- **Practice it as the related bebop dominant** over a `Dm7 – G7` vamp — one
  scale, two bars.
- **Then re-root it on D** and play it ascending and descending so you also hear
  it as a standalone minor sound, with the ♮3 passing through the ♭3.
- **Target the `Dm7` chord tones** (D, F, A, C) on the beats when you start the
  line from the ii rather than the V.
- **Keep the ♮3 chromatic** — it's a passing tone, not a Picardy third; don't
  rest on it.
