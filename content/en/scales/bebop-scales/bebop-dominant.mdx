The **bebop dominant** scale is the Mixolydian scale with one extra note: a
chromatic **natural 7th** wedged between the ♭7 and the root. That single passing
tone is the whole point — it turns a seven-note scale into an **eight-note**
scale, and eight even eighth-notes fill one bar of 4/4 exactly. Start on a chord
tone on beat 1 and the chord tones keep landing on the downbeats all the way
through.

### Interval formula

| Degree | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | ♭7 | 7 |
|--------|---|---|---|---|---|---|----|---|
| Semitones from root | 0 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 10 | **11** |

The note in bold is the chromatic passing tone. Compared to plain Mixolydian
(1 2 3 4 5 6 ♭7), you simply add the natural 7 — the leading tone of the key —
without removing anything.

### What it's played over

Use it over any **dominant 7th** chord — most often the **V7** of a key, or the
V7 in a ii–V–I. Over `G7`, play **G bebop dominant**.

### The rhythmic rule

This is what separates a bebop scale from any other scale. Run it in straight
eighth notes starting on a **chord tone** on beat 1, and every chord tone
(R, 3, 5, ♭7) falls on a **downbeat**; every non-chord tone falls on an upbeat.

Descending one octave of **G bebop dominant** over `G7`, starting on the root:

| Beat | 1 | & | 2 | & | 3 | & | 4 | & |
|------|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Note | G | F♯ | F | E | D | C | B | A |
| Role | **R** | *7* | **♭7** | 6 | **5** | 4 | **3** | 2 |

The chord tones **G, F, D, B** (R, ♭7, 5, 3) all land squarely on the beat. The
passing F♯ keeps the alignment intact — without it, the chord tones would drift
onto the upbeats and the line would sound "off."

### Practice tips

- **Descend from the root in eighths over a `G7` vamp** and feel the chord tones
  hit the downbeats. This is the single most important bebop exercise.
- **Start on each chord tone** (R, 3, 5, ♭7) on beat 1 and run down — each
  starting point keeps chord tones on the beats.
- **Don't add the passing tone going up the same way you do coming down** — when
  ascending, players usually insert the chromatic between 5 and 6 instead, or
  start the line so the chord tones still land on beats. Let your ear lead.
- **Apply it to a ii–V–I**: the V7 bar is where this scale lives. See also
  [Bebop Dorian](/lessons/scales/bebop-scales/bebop-dorian) for the ii chord.
