## Study Notes

Chord changes follow Miles Davis's recording on *Kind of Blue* (1959). Coltrane's **Impressions** uses the same form and changes.

### Modal, not functional

There are no ii–Vs: 16 bars of **D Dorian**, 8 bars of **Eb Dorian** (up a half step), then 8 more of D Dorian. The challenge is the opposite of Giant Steps — creating shape and tension over a single chord.

**A sections (Dm7):** **D Dorian** (D E F G A B C). The natural 6 (**B**) is the note that makes it Dorian rather than minor — feature it.

**B section (Ebm7):** **Eb Dorian** (Eb F Gb Ab Bb C Db). Make the half-step shift audible: start the bridge on a note that isn't in D Dorian (`Gb`, `Db` or `Eb`).

**Back to A:** Resolve the bridge back down a half step — `Gb → F`, `Db → C`, `Eb → D`.

### The "So What" voicing

Bill Evans's piano answers to the bass melody use a five-note voicing: **three 4ths plus a major 3rd** on top. On Dm7: `E A D G B` (from low to high). Moving that shape diatonically within D Dorian gives the familiar "So What chords"; move it up a half step for the bridge.

### Ideas for building a solo

- **Sidestepping:** briefly play a phrase a half step up (Eb Dorian over Dm7) and resolve back — creates tension without chord changes.
- **Pentatonics:** D minor, E minor and A minor pentatonic all sit inside D Dorian and give different colors.
- **Motifs:** Miles's solo develops a few short ideas with lots of space — transcribe his first chorus.

### Practice strategy

1. **Learn the bass melody** and the two-chord "So" – "What" answer.
2. **Quartal voicings:** play the So What voicing on every note of D Dorian, then Eb Dorian.
3. **Hear the form:** count 32 bars without a chart — knowing where the bridge starts is the whole game.
4. **Compare with Naima's pedal points:** another way of keeping one bass note while the colors above change.
