## Study Notes

Standard Real Book changes (credited to Miles Davis; often attributed to Eddie "Cleanhead" Vinson), as on *Cookin'* (1957), where it's played as a medley with "When Lights Are Low".

The whole tune is **ii–V–I in three keys, descending by whole step**: **D → C → Bb**, then back to D. It's the cleanest drill in the Real Book for changing keys quickly. It's also the base progression Coltrane reharmonized in **Countdown**.

### A — D major, then C major

**Bars 1–4 (Em7 / A7 / DMaj7):** ii–V–I in **D**. **D major** over all of it; guide tones `G → G → F# → F#`.

**Bars 5–8 (Dm7 / G7 / CMaj7):** ii–V–I in **C**, a whole step down. The Dm7 is a *parallel* move from DMaj7: only the 3rd (`F# → F`) and 7th (`C# → C`) change. Play those two notes to hear the key change.

### B — Bb major, pivot, and home

**Bars 9–11 (Cm7 / F7 / BbMaj7):** ii–V–I in **Bb**, same parallel move from CMaj7 (`E → Eb`, `B → Bb`).

**Bar 12 (EbMaj7):** IV of Bb — and a half step above the Em7 that follows. Use **Eb Lydian** (A natural), whose A anticipates A7. It works as a **chromatic pivot**: `EbMaj7 → Em7` is a half-step lift back toward D.

**Bars 13–16 (Em7 / A7 / DMaj7 / Em7 A7):** ii–V–I back in **D**, with a turnaround in bar 16.

### Practice strategy

1. **Three scales, three keys:** D major (bars 1–4, 13–16), C major (5–8), Bb major (9–12). Change scales on the ii chord, not on the I.
2. **Parallel-move drill:** play only the 3rds and 7ths from each IMaj7 to the next ii (bars 4 → 5, 8 → 9). Only two notes move, by half step.
3. **Tempo ladder:** start at 120 bpm with half notes, then quarters, then eighths. It's usually played fast.
4. **Then play Countdown:** same key centers with Coltrane's major-thirds cycle in between. Tune Up first makes Countdown much easier to hear.
