## Study Notes

Chord changes follow Shorter's original recording on *Adam's Apple* (1966). Bars 9–10 vary a lot between charts: the Real Book shows `D7(#9) | Db7(#11)`, and Miles Davis's *Miles Smiles* version uses a chain of ii–Vs. The chart here uses Shorter's own `F#m7b5 F7(#11) | E7alt A7(#9)`.

### Form and feel

A **12-bar minor blues in 6/4** — each bar is two groups of three beats over a repeating bass ostinato that outlines the tonic. It's the same skeleton as **Equinox** (i – iv – i – turnaround – i), but modal: the tonic chords are `m11` sounds rather than functional `m7`s.

**Bars 1–4 (Cm11):** **C Dorian**. The 11 (F) and 9 (D) are part of the sound — build lines and voicings in 4ths (e.g. `C F Bb Eb`).

**Bars 5–6 (Fm11):** **F Dorian**. Same quartal approach, a 4th up.

**Bars 7–8 (Cm11):** Back to C Dorian.

### Bars 9–10 — the turnaround

This is the heart of the tune: a chromatic, non-functional turnaround instead of the usual `Ab7 G7`.

**Bar 9 (F#m7b5 → F7(#11)):** **F# Locrian** (or F# Locrian #2), then **F Lydian dominant**. The roots slide down by half step.

**Bar 10 (E7alt → A7(#9)):** **E altered**, then **A altered** or **A half-whole diminished**. `A7 → Cm` is not a V–I; it lands on the tonic by voice leading (`C# → C`, `G → G`).

The bass line under bars 9–10 moves `F# → F → E → A`, then back to C. Learn it as a melody.

**Bars 11–12 (Cm11):** Tonic. Back to the ostinato.

### Practice strategy

1. **Sing the bass ostinato** and feel 6/4 as `1-2-3 4-5-6`.
2. **Quartal voicings** on Cm11 and Fm11 (stacked 4ths from the 11 or the root).
3. **Isolate bars 9–10:** loop the four chords slowly, targeting one guide tone per chord (`A → Eb → G# → C#`, then `C` on Cm11).
4. **Compare with Equinox and Mr. P.C.:** same form and key, three different turnarounds.
