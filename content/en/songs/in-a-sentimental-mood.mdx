## Study Notes

Simplified lead-sheet changes, a common reading of the version on *Duke Ellington & John Coltrane* (1963). Ellington's and Coltrane's actual voicings vary more than this chart; treat it as a skeleton and fill in by ear.

### A sections — line cliché and the relative major

**Bars 1–2 (Dm DmMaj7 / Dm7 Dm6):** The classic **minor line cliché**: the top note of the chord descends `D → C# → C → B` over a static D minor. Use **D melodic minor** on the first half (for the C#), **D Dorian** on the second (for the B).

**Bars 3–4 (Gm7 C7 / FMaj7):** ii–V–I into the **relative major**, F. **G Dorian → C Mixolydian → F major**. The tune lives between D minor and F major.

**Bar 5 (Em7b5 A7(b9)):** Minor ii–V back to D. **E Locrian / A half-whole diminished** (or D harmonic minor from A).

**Bars 6–8:** Line cliché again, then the turnaround (A1) or the ii–V into F (A2).

**Bar 16 (FMaj7 Ab7):** Ab7 is V of Db, setting up the bridge a half step above the D minor tonic. **Ab Mixolydian**.

### Bridge — Db major

**Bars 17–22 (DbMaj7 Bbm7 / Ebm7 Ab7):** A I–vi–ii–V loop in **Db major**, three times. One scale — **Db major** — covers all of it. The key is a half step above the D minor tonic: a dramatic but smooth lift.

**Bars 23–24 (Gm7 / Em7b5 A7(b9)):** Back to D minor: Gm7 (iv of D minor) then the minor ii–V. From Db, Gm7 is a tritone away — practice the voice leading so the move doesn't sound like a jump.

### Practice strategy

1. **Line cliché voicing:** keep D, F, A on the bottom and move only the top voice `D C# C B` — one voicing shape for bars 1–2.
2. **Two keys in the A:** mark D minor and F major areas and practice switching scale material at bar 3.
3. **Bridge in one key:** since it's all Db major, focus on melody and guide tones — connect the 7th of each chord to the 3rd of the next through the I–vi–ii–V loop.
4. **Listen to Coltrane's tone:** at ballad tempo, the melody and sound matter more than the number of notes.
