## Study Notes

Chart follows simplified Real Book–style changes; Bill Evans's *Explorations* (1961) recording reharmonizes freely, especially in the bridge. Treat this as the skeleton and check the bridge against the recording.

Nardis isn't a ii–V tune. Its sound comes from **E Phrygian** (F natural over E minor) pulling against **E harmonic minor** (D# in the B7).

### A sections

**Bar 1 (Em7):** Tonic. Start with **E Phrygian** (F natural) to set up the next bar — it's the tune's signature color.

**Bar 2 (FMaj7):** The bII chord — a Phrygian cadence sound. **F Lydian** (B natural) keeps you inside the E minor world. Voice-lead `E → F` in the top line.

**Bar 3 (B7):** Dominant. **E harmonic minor** from B (B Phrygian dominant). The D# is the leading tone — target it.

**Bar 4 (CMaj7):** Deceptive resolution: B7 goes to bVI instead of Em. **C Lydian** (F#) or C major.

**Bars 5–6 (Am7 / FMaj7):** iv → bII. **A Dorian** then **F Lydian** — note both share the natural `B` and `F`.

**Bars 7–8 (B7 / Em7):** V–i, this time resolving for real. **E harmonic minor → E Dorian/Aeolian**.

### B — bridge

Starts on the iv (Am7) and rests on Em7 for two bars before repeating the `Am7 → FMaj7 → B7 → Em` cadence. It's the calmest part of the tune — a good place for space and longer notes.

### Practice strategy

1. **Hear the Phrygian pull:** alternate `Em7 ↔ FMaj7` as a vamp and solo with E Phrygian only.
2. **Mark the D#:** every B7 bar, land on D# on beat 1 — it's what makes the minor key sound "Spanish".
3. **Listen to several Evans versions:** he played Nardis for 20 years; comparing *Explorations* with later live takes shows how the same skeleton gets reharmonized.
