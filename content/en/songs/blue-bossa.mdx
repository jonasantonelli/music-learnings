## Study Notes

Chart follows the standard Real Book changes, as recorded by Joe Henderson with Kenny Dorham on *Page One* (1963).

Two eight-bar halves: the first stays in **C minor**, the second makes a short trip to **Db major** and comes back with the same minor ii–V.

### A — C minor

**Bars 1–2 (Cm7):** Tonic. **C Dorian** or **C natural minor** — both work; Dorian's A natural gives a brighter bossa sound.

**Bars 3–4 (Fm7):** iv chord. **F Dorian** (or C natural minor from F). The guide-tone move `Bb → Ab` (7 of Cm7 → 3 of Fm7) is the melody's shape.

**Bar 5 (Dm7b5):** ii of the minor ii–V. **D Locrian** (C natural minor from D).

**Bar 6 (G7(b9)):** V. **C harmonic minor** from G (G Phrygian dominant) or **G altered**. Target B natural — the leading tone back to C.

**Bars 7–8 (Cm7):** Back to tonic.

### B — the Db detour

**Bars 9–10 (Ebm7 / Ab7):** ii–V to Db — a half step above the tonic. Use **Db major** across both bars (Eb Dorian → Ab Mixolydian).

**Bars 11–12 (DbMaj7):** The key-change moment. **Db major** or **Db Lydian** (G natural = the #11). Since Db is the bII of C minor, it sounds like a Neapolitan lift.

**Bars 13–14 (Dm7b5 / G7(b9)):** Same minor ii–V as bars 5–6, now doing the job of pulling you *out* of Db. The voice leading `Db → D` and `F → F` makes the return smooth.

**Bars 15–16 (Cm7 / Dm7b5 G7(b9)):** Tonic and turnaround.

### Practice strategy

1. **Switch scales at the key change:** C minor for bars 1–8, Db major for 9–12, C harmonic minor for 13–16. Say the scale name out loud the bar before it arrives.
2. **Guide tones only:** play 3rds and 7ths as half notes through the whole form.
3. **Compare with Solar:** both tunes tonicize a key a half/whole step away from C minor with a ii–V — Blue Bossa is the gentler version.
