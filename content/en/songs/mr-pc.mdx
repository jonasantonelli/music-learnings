## Study Notes

Chord changes follow Coltrane's recording on *Giant Steps* (1960) — the standard minor blues. The tune is named for bassist Paul Chambers.

The form is the same as **Equinox**, in the same key; only the tempo is very different (Mr. P.C. is played at about ♩ = 250+). Material you've worked out on Equinox transfers directly — the challenge is keeping it clear at speed.

**Bars 1–4 (Cm7):** Tonic. **C Dorian**, or **C minor pentatonic** / **C blues** for the bluesy side. At this tempo, clear 8th-note lines built from Cm7 arpeggios with chromatic approaches work better than long scale runs.

**Bars 5–6 (Fm7):** iv chord. **F Dorian**. The move to listen for is `G (5 of Cm) → Ab (3 of Fm)`, and back down in bar 7.

**Bars 7–8 (Cm7):** Back to tonic. Many players insert a `Dm7b5 G7` in bar 8 to set up bar 9 — optional.

**Bar 9 (Ab7):** bVI7, a half step above the V. **Ab Lydian dominant** (Ab Bb C D Eb F Gb) — the D natural and C sit well against the C minor sound.

**Bar 10 (G7alt):** V. **G altered** (Ab Bb B C# D# F) or **C harmonic minor** from G. Resolve `Ab → G` or `B → C` into bar 11.

**Bars 11–12 (Cm7 | G7alt):** Tonic, then the turnaround dominant. A common alternative in bars 11–12 is `Cm7 Ab7 | Dm7b5 G7alt`.

### Practice strategy

1. **Slow first:** play it at the Equinox tempo, then increase in steps of 10 bpm.
2. **Guide tones:** `Eb → Ab → Eb → Gb → B → Eb` (3rds of Cm7, Fm7, Cm7, 7th of Ab7, 3rd of G7, 3rd of Cm7) — one note per bar at full tempo.
3. **Learn the head:** the melody is a single riff-like line built on the minor blues — play it in all 12 keys to own the form.
