## Study Notes

Chart follows common Real Book–style changes, as played by Chet Baker on *Chet* (1959). Versions vary most in bars 9–14 of each A — check them against the recording.

The form is the lesson here: **A sections are 14 bars**, not 8, so the full chorus is 50 bars. Count carefully until the long A feels natural.

### A sections

**Bars 1–4 (Dm6 / Em7b5 A7(b9)):** Tonic vamp with a minor ii–V back to itself. On Dm6 use **D Dorian** or **D melodic minor** (the B natural of Dm6 is the key color). On the ii–V use **D harmonic minor** across the whole bar (E Locrian ♮2 → A Phrygian dominant).

**Bar 6 (Am7b5 D7(b9)):** Minor ii–V to the iv chord, Gm7. **G harmonic minor** across the bar.

**Bars 7–8 (Gm7):** iv chord. **G Dorian**.

**Bars 9–10 (Bm7 E7 / Gm7 C7):** Two ii–Vs a minor third apart. The first (`Bm7 E7`) is a surprise — it's a deceptive side-step (think E7 → A, never resolved); the second (`Gm7 C7`) is the real one, landing on F. Use **A major** on Bm7–E7 and **F major** on Gm7–C7.

**Bar 11 (FMaj7):** Relative major — a brief bright moment. **F major**.

**Bars 12–14:** Minor ii–Vs back to D minor. At the end of A2, `Am7b5 D7(b9)` pivots into the bridge's Gm7.

### B — bridge

**Bars 1–4 (Gm7 / Gm7b5 C7(b9)):** Starts on iv, then the Gm7 turns half-diminished and becomes ii of F. **G Dorian → F harmonic minor** (or C altered on C7(b9)).

**Bars 5–6 (FMaj7):** Relative major again — **F major**.

**Bars 7–8 (Em7b5 / A7(b9)):** Minor ii–V back to D minor for the last A.

### Last A

Bars 9–10 replace the side-step ii–Vs with a plain `Em7b5 → A7(b9)`, so the tune winds down to D minor without the trip to F.

### Practice strategy

1. **Count the form out loud** once through with just roots — 14 + 14 + 8 + 14.
2. **One scale per ii–V:** D harmonic minor, G harmonic minor, A major, F major. Four scales cover the whole tune.
3. **Compare with Beautiful Love:** same key and same `Em7b5 A7(b9) → Dm` cadence — lift your Beautiful Love lines straight into bars 2 and 4.
