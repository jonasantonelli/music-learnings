## Study Notes

Standard Real Book changes; the reference is Miles Davis's live *My Funny Valentine* (1964), with Herbie Hancock on piano.

Same composer as Beautiful Love, and the same opening move: a **half-diminished ii–V that doesn't resolve where you expect**. The tune is through-composed (ABCD, no repeated section) and only reaches its tonic, **BbMaj7, in bar 9**. Half the work is keeping track of where you are.

### A — the long way to the tonic

**Bars 1–2 (Em7b5 / A7(b9)):** Minor ii–V to D minor — which never arrives. **E Locrian** (or E Locrian #2), then **A half-whole diminished** / **A Mixolydian b9 b13**.

**Bars 3–4 (Cm7 / F7):** Instead, a ii–V to Bb... **C Dorian** / **F Mixolydian**.

**Bars 5–6 (Fm7 / Bb7):** ...which also slides away: ii–V to **Eb**. **F Dorian** / **Bb Mixolydian**.

**Bars 7–8 (EbMaj7 / Ab7):** IV, then **backdoor bVII7** leading to BbMaj7. **Eb Lydian** (A natural) → **Ab Lydian dominant** (D natural).

### B — tonic, then out to F

**Bar 9 (BbMaj7):** First tonic. **Bb major**.

**Bars 10–11 (Em7b5 A7 / Dm7):** Minor ii–V–i to **D minor** (iii of Bb) — the resolution the opening denied. **D Aeolian** or **D Dorian**.

**Bar 12 (Bbm7 Eb7):** ii–V to Ab — it doesn't go there. Treat it as a chromatic side-step (or a backdoor to F).

**Bar 13 (FMaj7):** Brief tonicization of **F major** (V of Bb as a key area).

**Bars 14–16 (Em7b5 A7 / Am7b5 / D7(b9)):** Two minor ii–Vs. The first points to D minor again; the second (`Am7b5 D7`) leads to G.

### C — dominant pedal, minor iv

**Bars 17–18 (G7alt):** The climax — two bars of V/ii. **G altered** or **G half-whole diminished**. The melody reaches its high point here.

**Bars 19–20 (Cm7):** Resolves to ii of Bb. **C Dorian**.

**Bars 21–22 (Ab7):** bVII7 — backdoor again. **Ab Lydian dominant**.

**Bars 23–24 (BbMaj7):** Tonic.

### D — chain of minor ii–Vs

**Bars 25–30:** Three half-diminished ii–Vs descending by whole step: to **Dm**, **Cm**, **Bb**. Each uses **Locrian** on the m7b5 and **half-whole diminished** (or Mixolydian b9 b13) on the dominant.

**Bars 31–32 (BbMaj7):** Final tonic; the top of the form restarts on Em7b5.

### Practice strategy

1. **Name the targets:** write the key each ii–V points to (Dm, Bb, Eb, Bb, Dm, F, G, Cm, Bb...) and play just those tonic triads.
2. **Half-diminished drill:** practice m7b5 → 7(b9) in all keys used here (E, D, C, A). Most bars in the tune are one of these.
3. **Guide tones:** the 3rd/7th line through bars 1–8 moves by half step almost every bar — play it as whole notes.
4. **Listen to Miles and Herbie:** the 1964 version uses rubato and reharmonization. Compare it to the chart to hear which changes they keep.
